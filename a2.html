Health Display Prototypes

My first area of exploration is how Sirens.io represents health. As a back-end tool, its interface needs to show the DM how much health is left at a glance during a session. I designed my health prototypes across a variety of styles to work out which way of representing this information suits that kind of interface best.

protoHealth1: Vertical Bar

My first prototype is a basic, standard health bar, based on how health is shown across a wide variety of media. It works almost as a benchmark. By including the typical interface, I can see how testers receive it compared to the more stylised versions in protoHealth2 and protoHealth3.

protoHealth1

protoHealth2: Circle

My second prototype takes more inspiration from video game interfaces, changing the bar into a ring that empties clockwise from the top. I'm curious to see whether users view the circle as more or less appealing than the typical vertical bar.

protoHealth2

protoHealth3: Heart

My third prototype works very similarly to my first, but uses the shape of a heart instead, with the health points draining from it like liquid from a glass. Because the heart is wider at the top, the red area shrinks faster than the HP in the first half, and I'm interested to see whether testers notice this.

protoHealth3

Current Bar Prototypes

My second area of exploration is how best to convey time and musical beats in a visual interface. Both prototypes run at 140 BPM, with the section colour changing every 4 bars to stand in for the music changing. I was curious to find out whether simpler, more stripped-back UIs would be favoured over more detailed and informative ones.

protoBar1: Original Conveyor

My first prototype has the basic conveyor function, with the bars moving from right to left under a fixed playhead. For me, this UI is enough on its own: as soon as I see the dots move from right to left twice, I can infer the tempo of the song. However, it may not be enough for others who aren't as musically inclined.

protoBar1

protoBar2: Bouncing Arrow

My second prototype builds on the first by adding a bouncing arrow, which lands on the current bar each time to help mark out the pacing of the song. I took inspiration from karaoke interfaces, which highlight the lyrics at a set rate to show how fast you should be singing a line.
