Information Design Prototypes

My first area of exploration is related to the information design of my sequencer. While there were multiple elements whose specific information design went through iterative stages, such as the note input box, I felt that the area which would benefit most from feedback from my peers would be the overall layout of the sequencer steps. While modern DAWs have somewhat solidified the western musical notation understanding of writing notes, from left to right, there exist a few different lineages of hardware and software sequencers who approach this from different angles.

I am concerned with a couple of different aspects related to the layout's information design. Firstly, approachability - how does its initial readability allow for users to conceptualise the steps. Beyond first impressions I am also concerned with how it helps users to explore the loop as a repeated structure, especially in line with the reset functionality. One element that is not directly communicated/tested within the prototypes, but might be important to future development is scalability - how might the layout accommodate extra steps or controls per step.

Prototype 1: Vertical Layout

This prototype was my initial design, inspired by audio tracker software. I suspect that the verticality was more to do with the GUI limitations of the early hardware, but this is what got me interested in exploring design the area. The amount of text involved in a single step can more easily be displayed in this orientation, rather than having to turn your head to read this if it was laid out horizontally.

Prototype 2: Horizontal Layout

This prototype was my first variation, moving from vertical to the more typical left to right horizontal orientation of DAWs and western musical notation. As noted above, the amount of information displayed per step would really change the feel of using this. This isn't a particularly novel layout to explore but it provides a helpful reference point to the other two.

Prototype 3: Circular Layout

Finally, we have the more adventurous circular layout, based on the Future Retro Orb Sequencer . As mentioned in the area introduction, I'm interested in conceptualising loops as a repetitive structure, so borrowing from the layout of something like a clock makes sense here - the last step is now next to the first rather than the furtherest from it.
